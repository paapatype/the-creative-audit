03Questions
Q1Our team already did an internal audit.+
Good — that's the inside-out view: what you have, honestly catalogued. The Creative Audit is the outside-in view: your market, your competitors, the global standard, and a visual direction non-designers can see and approve. One tells you what exists. The other tells you what to do. You need both; you've only done one.
Q2I just need packaging or a website. Can you quote that directly?+
I can — but one redesigned box without a strategy underneath is an orphan. It won't match the rest of your shelf, your team will ask what system it follows, and you'll pay to redo it. If you'd like to pay for the same packaging twice, I'm happy to oblige. The audit exists so you don't have to.
Q3Is this just a funnel into your design services?+
The plan you walk away with is portable by design — it works with any designer, anywhere, and it says so on every page. If you want me to build it, or to place you with a studio I trust, that door is open. But the audit's value stands on its own. That independence is why you can trust the advice.
Q4What do I actually get at the end?+
One plain-language presentation, plus a PDF you can keep and circulate: your positioning, a competitive read, the global standard for your category, a visual direction with the reasoning attached, and a sequenced, priced plan you can hand to any designer.
Q5How long does it take?+
Two weeks from the discovery call to the presentation. Faster if you genuinely need it — but two weeks is enough to do it properly, and not so long that it stalls your momentum.
Q6What happens after the audit?+
You'll have a clear recommendation for next steps — what to fix first, in what order, at what cost. Acting on it with me is optional; the plan is yours to take anywhere. Most people come back because they'd rather build with the person who already understands the brief.
Q7What kind of businesses is this for?+
Founder-led brands at an inflection — product, manufacturing, D2C, services. If you make something and your brand no longer fits where the business is going, it fits. If you only need a quick logo and have no appetite for strategy, it doesn't.
