How much should a logo cost — and why it's the wrong question

By SankalpThe Creative Audit

A logo can cost ₹2,000 on a marketplace or ₹5 lakh from a studio, and both are "correct." If that sentence is frustrating, good — it points straight at the real problem.

A logo is not a brand. It's the smallest, most visible piece of a much larger decision about who you are and who you're for. Buying a logo without that decision is like buying a front door before you've designed the house — you'll get something, and it almost certainly won't fit.

Why the same thing costs a hundred times more

A cheap logo is a drawing. An expensive one is the output of research, positioning, and a system designed to flex across everything you make — packaging, website, signage, the lot. You're not paying for the mark itself. You're paying for the thinking that makes that mark the right one, and the system that keeps it coherent everywhere it appears.

You're not buying a logo. You're buying a decision about who you are — the logo is just where it shows up first.

The trap founders fall into

People fixate on logo cost because it's the one design thing they can picture. But a beautiful logo sitting on top of confused positioning is lipstick on a question mark. It won't fix what's actually losing you customers — a brand that doesn't communicate what you do, why you're different, or why anyone should trust you with their money.

What to do instead

Get the decision right first. Know your position, your competition, and what you want the brand to communicate. Then a logo — at almost any budget — has a target to hit, and your money buys craft instead of guesswork. The question was never "how much should a logo cost." It's "do we actually know what this logo is supposed to say?"

That decision — sharp, evidenced, and portable — is exactly what the audit hands you before you commission a single pixel.
